Casement vs Double-Hung Windows: An In-Depth Showdown

Comparing ventilation aerodynamics, air-tightness seals, ease of cleaning, and architectural styling between the two most popular replacement window styles.

Aesthetic Heritage vs Contemporary Minimalism

Double-hung windows are the quintessential American architectural style, found across Colonial, Victorian, and Craftsman homes. With vertical sliding sashes and classic divided-lite grilles, they preserve period authenticity. Casement windows, with their unhindered full-sheet glass and outward swing, deliver sleek European minimalism and uninterrupted garden sightlines.

The Engineering Showdown: Air Infiltration Rates

When it comes to pure thermal airtightness, casement windows hold a distinct mechanical advantage:

A double-hung window relies on sliding weatherstripping brush seals along jamb tracks. While modern materials are effective, winter wind pressure can slightly pull sashes away from meeting rails. In contrast, a casement window features continuous rubber bulb compression gaskets. When you turn the lock handle, stainless steel cams draw the sash tightly against the frame like a refrigerator door, achieving an air leakage rate up to 8x tighter than sliding windows.

Ventilation Aerodynamics

Because double-hung sashes overlap, a maximum of 50% of the window area can be opened at any one time. Casement windows open a full 90 degrees, offering 100% top-to-bottom ventilation. Furthermore, an open casement sash acts as a wind scoop, catching parallel breezes and redirecting fresh air directly into the home.

Fig 1.5 — 90-degree outward swing casement scoop ventilation versus dual-sash vertical convection airflow in residential homes.

Key Takeaways for Homeowners

  • Always demand certified NFRC independent ratings rather than relying solely on manufacturer claims.
  • In Northern climates, prioritize a low U-Factor (≤ 0.22) and tight air infiltration (< 0.02 CFM/ft²).
  • Professional installation with continuous closed-cell foam and sloped sill pans is essential for lasting performance.
